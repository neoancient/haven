# Actions
When players take action, dice are used any time there is interesting opposition. Landing a shuttle, using magic to
start a fire, or searching a room do not require a dice roll and the results can be narrated with as much
or as little detail as desired. Trying to land a shuttle in severe weather conditions, magically lighting a fire
during a downpour with fuel that is soaked, or searching a room under tight time constraints all involve opposition, and
usually require a skill check. If failure would not add anything to the story, the opposition is not interesting.
In that case, there is no reason to make a skill check, and the opposition can be addressed by having the player
describe how the character handles the difficulty.

## Skill Checks
When a character takes an action that requires a skill check, follow these steps.
- Determine which skill is most appropriate.
- Roll four fate dice.  For each +, add one. For each - subtract one.
- Add the total to the skill rating.
- If the player opts to invoke an aspect, either add two to the result or reroll the dice (player's option).

## Opposition
The result of the player's roll is compared to the opposition. The opposition can be active or passive. When another
character is in a position to oppose the character's action, the opposition is determined by the opposing character's
skill check. When the opposition is passive, the gamemaster sets a rating on the ladder as the opposition.

## Outcomes
There are four possible outcomes to the skill check.
- Fail: If the roll is lower than the opposition, the action fails. That may be all there is, or the failure may create a complication. There may be a chance to succeed by accepting a serious cost.
- Tie: If the roll is the same as the opposition, there is a tie. You might get a lesser version of what you wanted, or you get it at a minor cost.
- Succeed: If the roll is one or two greater than the opposition, the action succeeds with no cost.
- Succeed with Style: If the result is three or more higher than the opposition, the action succeeds and you get an added benefit.

# The Four Actions
There are four types of actions: overcome, create advantage, attack, and defend. Often the type of action will be obvious
from the situation, but there are times when a player may choose to use a skill to do something unusual. For instance,
**Shoot** is nearly always used to attack an opponent (an attack action), but it could be used to pin down an
opponent with suppressing fire (create advantage) or to shoot a rope on the other side of the moat to drop the
drawbridge (overcome).

## Overcome
The overcome action deals with something between the character and their goal. One can use **Burglary** to pick a lock,
**Mechanics** to fix a malfunctioning device, **Empathy** to calm a crowd, for example. This is the catch-all action when
another does not fit.
- Failure means you can choose between not getting what you want or getting what you want at a serious cost.
- A tie means you get what you want at a minor cost.
- Success means you get what you want without a cost.
- Success with style means you get what you want, plus a boost.

## Create Advantage
A create advantage action is an attempt to act on something in the scene that gives you an advantage. Success will either
create a situational aspect on the scene or a character (e.g. overturning stalls when being chased trough a crowded market),
discover an existing aspect that was previously unknown (usually using **Notice**, or **Empathy** if discovering a character
aspect), or position the character to take advantage of an existing situation. Success gives the character a free invoke
on the aspect.
- Failure means the aspect is not created or discovered, or it is created but the opponent gets the free invoke.
- A tie means that when trying to create an aspect, you create a boost instead. If attempting to act on an existing aspect, you get a free invoke.
- Success means you create the aspect if it did not previously exist, and you get one free invoke.
- Success with style means that you get two free invokes instead of one.

# Attack
An attack is an attempt to harm an opponent or take them out. Opposition is always active unless the adversary is unaware
of the attack or not important enough to bother with dice.
- Failure means no damage is done to target.
- A tie does not damage the target, but you gain a boost.
- Success inflicts hits on the opponent equals to the number of shifts.
- Success with style inflicts hits on the opponent equal to the number of shifts, but can be reduced by one to gain a boost.

# Defend
A defend action is the active opposition to an attack action.
- Failure means that you take hits from the successful attack.
- A tie inflicts no damage on you, but the attacker gains a boost.
- Success avoids any damage.
- Success with style avoids damage, and the defender gains a boost.
