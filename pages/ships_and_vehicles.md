# Ships and Vehicles

## Overview
Ships and vehicles are mechanical devices that allow movement at greater speed than on foot or in
hostile environments (often both). They are treated as characters, with aspects, a reduced set of skills,
a physical stress track, and possibly stunts and extras. In this section, the word *ships* will be used, but unless
otherwise indicated, everything stated about ships applies to vehicles. These rules can also be applied to creatures
used as mounts.

## Skills
Ships have a subset of character skills. When a character performs an action using the ship's systems, the character's
skill is modified by +1 if the ship has a higher skill level, or -1 if the ship has a lower skill level.
Example: A character with a **Pilot** skill of Great (+4) flying a clunky freighter with a **Pilot** skill of Average (+1)
would be treated as Good (+3). The same pilot in a starfighter with a **Pilot** skill of Fantastic (+6) would
effectively be Superb (+5).

### Mechanics
**Mechanics** is used for repairing ship systems and making modifications. Ships can begin recovery from consequences
with a successful mechanics check, but the length of repair is the same as what characters require to remove consequences.

### Notice
**Notice** is used for the ship's sensors. It can be used to detect ships at a distance, scan a ship or other object (in
which case the character should use **Investigate**, modified by the ship's **Notice**). It can also be used to
create an advantage by detecting something in the scene, much like the standard use of **Notice**.

### Physique
**Physique** reflects the ship's durability and engine power. **Physique** can be used defensively to soak damage. It can also
be used when brute force is required, such as breaking free of a tractor beam. A ship with Average (+1) or Fair (+2)
**Physique** gets a 3-box hull stress track. A ship with Good (+3) or Great (+4) **Physique** gets a 4-box hull stress
track. A ship with Superb (+5) or higher **Physique** gets a 4-box stress track and an extra mild consequence slot.

### Pilot
**Pilot** reflects the ship's speed and maneuverability. It takes the place of Athletics, and can be used to sprint
or to dodge incoming fire, or in a contest when attempting to flee or close with another ship.

### Science
**Science** reflects the ship's computer systems. It can be used for such things as retrieving data from the Omni or
decrypting a code.

### Shoot
**Shoot** reflects the fire control systems and is used to fire the ship's weapons. It is used for Attack actions. 

### Star Drive
**Star Drive** is used when attempting to move into or out of astral space. A skill check should only be required in
unusual situations. The skill is also the maximum depth the ship is capable of.

### Stealth
**Stealth** is mostly about electronic signals. It can be used for such things as reducing the ship's electronic signature,
jamming the sensors of other ships, or hiding in a debris field.

### Will
**Will** reflects the resilience of a ship's systems. It can be used to defend against an attack such as an ion or EMP blast,
or even magical disruption. It can be used to oppose an enemy attempt to jam or access systems.
A ship with Average (+1) or Fair (+2) Physique gets a 3-box system stress track. A ship with Good (+3) or Great (+4) gets
a 4-box system stress track. A ship with Superb (+5) or higher PHysique gets a 4-box system stress track and an extra mild
consequence slot.

## Stress Tracks
Like characters, ships have two stress tracks, which default to two boxes. High skills can add additional boxes as with
characters. Ship size class can also add boxes to to either or both tracks.

### Hull Stress
The Hull stress track corresponds to the physical stress track. It is used for physical damage to the ship, such as
damage from collisions or most combat damage.

### System Stress
The system stress track corresponds to the mental stress track. It represents the resilience of the ship's systems.
Attempts to overload or disrupt ship systems do system stress. Magical disruption also does system stress.

## Consequences
Ships have the same consequence slots as characters. As with characters, the consequence is an aspect that can be
invoked against the ship. Recovery uses the same timeline as character consequences, except that recovery usually begins
with a Mechanics check.

## Ship Weapons
Ships can be equipped with a variety of weapons, from laser turrets to plasma cannons to disruptor torpedoes, to whatever
else sounds large and destructive. For purposes of play, all weapons are either standard or capital.
**Standard** weapons are the type found on smaller ships and ground vehicles. Their range is limited to the same zone
in space, or one zone on the ground or in the atmosphere. They are effective against smaller targets, but have
difficulty penetrating the armor of larger capital ships.
**Capital** weapons are only found on capital ships or fortified ground installations. Capital weapons have a range of
one zone in space or two zones on the ground or in an atmosphere. Designed to handle the defenses of slower-moving capital
ships, they have difficulty tracking smaller ships with greater speed and maneuverability. If they do manage to hit, it can
be devastating; capital weapons gain Weapon:2.

Because ships have crew to handle multiple weapons installations, they can make multiple attacks during the same
exchange. The number of attacks of each type is based on the ship's size class.

## Stunts
Stunts for ships are as versatile as stunts for characters. Some examples of ship-specific stunts:
- **Fighter/Bomber:** This fighter or small craft is equipped with missiles with capital-scale warheads. It can make a capital attack instead of a standard attack.
- **Tractor Beam:** This capital ship can forego one standard attack to trap another ship in the same zone. This is gain advantage action using the attacker's **Shoot** skill, opposed by the target's **Pilot** skill. If successful, the target gains the "Caught in a Tractor Beam" aspect. The target may not change zones while caught. The tractor lock can be broken with an overcome, using the trapped ship's **Pilot** or **Physique** opposed by the trapping ship's **Physique**.
- **Non-Standard Star Drive:** The ship can move to non-standard depths in astral space, to try to avoid detection. This is illegal in some regions, including the Yrth Empire, and looked on with suspicion in others.
- **Advanced Sensors:** +2 to **Notice** when scanning at long range.
- **Overcharged Reactors:** Once per scene, add +2 to **Shoot** but take 1 systems stress.
- **Stealth Hull:** +2 to **Stealth** against standard sensors.
- **Reinforced Bulkheads:** +2 to **Physique** when defending against collisions or ramming.

## Astral Travel
Astral space allows ships to travel faster relative to normal space. Though it is not dimensional travel, moving into astral
space is normally thought of as downward movement, and returning to normal space is thought of as upward. The "deeper" one
goes into astral space, the faster one travels relative to normal space. There are a standard set of depths, which are given
numerical values, though these numbers do not mean anything to anyone without an advanced degree in multiple disciplines
of mathematics, physics, and magic. The maximum depth a ship can reach is determined by its **Star Drive** skill.

Astral travel can also be achieved manually by magic, though most people would consider it reckless. Moving into or out of
astral space belongs to the **Movement** realm of magic. It is an overcome action, with the number of shifts generated on a
successful roll providing the maximum depth. If the roll results is success with style, the ship can be moved to any non-standard
depth up to the total number of shifts. Use of magic carries the normal risk to the ship's systems.

Most interstellar traffic occurs between settled systems, which have astral gates. A ship can pass through the gate to enter
astral space to a set depth, then at the destination the ship passes through another astral gate to reenter normal space.
Many transports and freighters do not have star drives, and instead rely on astral gates.

When transit times are needed, assume one day of transit time per light year of distance at depth 1. At greater depths,
determine the time units (e.g. hours, days, months, years) and characterize the number of units as several, a few, one, or half.
For each depth beyond 1, reduce the number of units. After half, go to a smaller unit and start again with several.
Example: A trip of 87 light years would take 87 days at depth one, which is equivalent to a few months. Going deeper in the astral
results in the following transit times:
1: a few months
2: a month
3: half a month
4: several days
5: a few days
6: one day
7: half a day
8: several hours
etc.

## Size Class
The size of a ship can have a significant impact on maneuverability and ability to take damage, beyond what can easily be
reflected by skills.

### Small Craft [0 refresh]
Small craft are smaller than capital ships, but large enough to have several crew and facilities for extended operations.
Examples include small freighters, patrol craft, cutters, and long-distance shuttles. They have crew quarters for up to about a dozen passengers
and crew, but may be crewed by as few as a single pilot. Most ground vehicles also fall into this category,
as do most creature mounts.
- May make 1 standard attack per exchange.
- Defends at +1 against capital weapon attacks.

### Fighters [1 refresh]
Fighters are small ships built to be fast and highly maneuverable. They usually have a single pilot, though some also have
a seat for a gunner, navigator, or electronic warfare officer.
- May make 1 standard attack per exchange.
- Notice is at +2 for determining turn order in combat.
- All **Pilot** checks are made at +1 except sprinting, which is +2.
- Add +2 to **Stealth** checks involving movement or dependent on size (such as hiding in a debris field).
- Defends at +2 against capital weapon attacks.

### Capital Ships
Capital ships range from the corvettes and escorts up to battleships. They are named after naval combat ships, but the
same rules can be used for other ships like bulk freighters, heavy transports, or space stations of similar size.
#### Corvettes [1 refresh]
- May make 2 standard attacks or 1 capital attack per exchange.
- Notice is at -1 for determining turn order in combat.
- Successful attacks against a smaller target based on Physique have the Weapon:1 quality
- One additional box of hull stress.
#### Frigates [2 refresh]
- May make 2 standard attacks and 2 capital attacks per exchange.
- Notice is at -2 for determining turn order in combat.
- Successful attacks against a smaller target based on Physique have the Weapon:2 quality
- Armor:1 against hull stress from non-capital ship weapons.
- Two additional boxes of hull stress.
- One additional box of system stress.
#### Destroyers/Cruisers [3 refresh]
- May make 2 standard attacks and 3 capital attacks per exchange (cruiser) or 4 standard and 2 capital (destroyer).
- Notice is at -4 for determining turn order in combat.
- Successful attacks against a smaller target based on Physique have the Weapon:4 quality
- Armor:2 against hull stress from non-capital ship weapons.
- Four additional boxes of hull stress.
- Two additional boxes of system stress.
#### Battleships [4 refresh]
- May make 4 standard attacks and 6 capital attacks per exchange.
- Notice is at -6 for determining turn order in combat.
- Successful attacks against a smaller target based on Physique have the Weapon:6 quality
- Armor:3 against hull stress from non-capital ship weapons.
- Six additional boxes of hull stress.
- Three additional boxes of system stress.
