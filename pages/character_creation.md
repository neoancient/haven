# Character Aspects
Every character has five character aspects. Two of these are essential to the character concept and should be determined
at the beginning of the process. The others can be determined later, possibly even during the first session.
The first two character aspects are the **High Concept** and the **Trouble**.

The **High Concept** is a basic summary of
the character, such as _Cantankerous old Wizard_ or _Bounty Hunter with a Heart of Gold_. Mixing roles can make
memorable characters, such as _Wizard Bounty Hunter_ or _Troubadour Mechanic_.

The **Trouble** aspect is what tends to add complications to the character's life. It could be an outside force,
like _Yrth Navy Wants Me Dead_ or an internal struggle, like _What's One More Drink?_

The remaining three aspects can be anything that helps round out the character. The best aspects have both
positive and negative sides. Aspects are a way to tell the gamemaster what sorts of things you want to have
come up in the story. For more information on selecting aspects, see [Aspects]({{ site.baseurl }}/aspects.html).

# Approaches to Character Creation
The steps given so far assume a traditional approach to creating a character, in which the beginning character details
are fully developed, but it is possible to defer some character creation and develop during play.
## Session Zero
A session zero can be used to develop the characters' backgrounds and their relationships with the other characters. When
using a session zero, only the high concept and trouble are determined beforehand. Each player writes a brief description
of their first true adventure, and creates an aspect based on it. The stories are then redistributed, and the players
each give themselves a role in another character's story, whether to render assistance, or add a complication, or otherwise
affect the way the story goes. The player then creates an aspect based on that role. The last step is then
repeated (assuming at least three players), with the caveat that no character can contribute to the same story more than once.
## Deferring Details Until Session One
Another approach is to defer some or all skills, stunts, or aspects (other than the _High Concept_ and the _Trouble_) until
the first session. When a skill is first used, the player can decide where it goes on the character sheet and write it in.
If a situation suggests a stunt that seems like it fits the character, the player adds it to the sheet. Aspects likewise
may suggest themselves through interaction with the setting.
