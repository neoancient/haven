In the Fate system, character creation is not something that is done before playing. It is the beginning of playing. The
character process tells the character's story to the current point and is collaborative. Some details can even be deferred
and filled in as needed during the first scenario.

The following sections touch on each step of the process, but each step has its own page that gives more detail. Each step
is illustrated by examples of Annie and Fizzbin, two NPCs that are part of Haven.

# Character Aspects
Every character has five character aspects. Two of these are essential to the character concept and should be determined
at the beginning of the process. The others can be determined later, possibly even during the first session.
The first two character aspects are the **High Concept** and the **Trouble**.

The **High Concept** is a basic summary of
the character, such as _Cantankerous old Wizard_ or _Bounty Hunter with a Heart of Gold_. Mixing roles can make
memorable characters, such as _Wizard Bounty Hunter_ or _Troubadour Mechanic_.

The **Trouble** aspect is what tends to add complications to the character's life. It could be an outside force,
like _Yrth Navy Wants Me Dead_ or an internal struggle, like _What's One More Drink?_

The remaining three aspects can be anything that helps round out the character. The best aspects have both
positive and negative sides. Aspects are a way to tell the gamemaster what sorts of things you want to have
come up in the story. For more information on selecting aspects, see [Aspects]({{ site.baseurl }}/aspects.html).

_**Example:** Emily wants to play a lovable street urchin. She names her character Annie, taking the name from the comic
strip character Little Orphan Annie. She starts with a high concept of **Street Urchin**, then thinks about how to make it
more interesting. She wants Annie to be good at getting herself out of trouble, so she expands it to **Clever Street Urchin.**
Because Annie's cleverness is shown in getting out of trouble, it implies that she gets into trouble, so she finishes up
her high concept with **Clever Street Urchin with a Penchant for Trouble**. For Annie's trouble, Emily thinks about how
she became a street urchin in the first place. There are many ways Annie could have become an orphan, but Emily decides
that maybe she has absent parent. Subconsciously borrowing from the story of Pippi Longstocking, Emily decides that Annie's
father is frequently away for long periods as captain of a pirate ship, but he will be coming back. Other people in Haven
believe that she is truly an orphan that has managed to fool herself into think that she still has a living parent. Annie's
trouble is **My Father Is Coming Back Someday**. She holds off on the other aspects, waiting to see what the rest of
the party comes up with to help give her ideas._

_Jim has a very clear idea of the sort of character he wants to play: a goblin that specializes in explosions. He doesn't
have to think very hard to come up with **Explosive Engineer Extraordinaire** as his high concept and **Prone to "Testing"
in the Wrong Places** as his trouble. He comes up with a couple more aspects at this stage as well: **I've Got a Bomb for
That** and **I've Built Worse Things on Purpose** He reasons that **I've Got a Bomb for That** is useful for enhancing the
effectiveness of his explosions, but could also indicate a tendency to see blowing things up as a viable solution in nearly
any circumstance. **I've Built Worse Things on Purpose** can be invoked to find faults in other people's work compelled
to find faults with his own. Jim still has one aspect left to create, but waits until he and Emily have worked out some
of the details of their past interactions. Jim names his character Fizzbin, a reference from an episode of the original
series of Star Trek._

# Species and Culture
Select the character's species or background. Select the three cultural stunts. You may want to wait until after skills
are assigned for selecting the cultural stunts. See [Species and Culture]({{ site.baseurl }}/species_and_culture.html)

_**Example:** Emily decides Annie is a human. She looks through the list of backgrounds but does not find one that reflects
Annie's experience as a street urchin. Working with the GM, Emily comes up with three situations in which she can use her
culture skill to replace the skill that would normally be used: for **Contacts** when dealing beggars or other street urchins,
for **Physique** when resisting disease or malnutrition, and instead of **Empathy** to read a crowd._

_Jim has already decided on Goblin, and selects stunts which allow him to substitute **Culture** for **Athletics** when
fleeing destruction of his own creation, **Investigate** when evaluating broken, half-finished, or nonsensical devices,
and **Provoke** when escalating chaos, drawing attention, or triggering a cascade of unintended effects._

# Skills
Characters start with ten skills on the character sheet: one at Great (+4), two at Good (+3), three at Fair (+2),
and four at Average (+1). All other skills are considered Mediocre (+0). At various milestones there are opportunities
to shift skills around and increase them. See [Advancement]({{ site.baseurl }}/advancement.html)

_**Example:** Emily has decided that Annie, though only in her mid teens, is a mother figure for younger orphans. She has
an easy time getting people to like an trust her. She selects **Rapport** at Great (+4) and **Empathy** at Good (+3). She
also reasons that she needs to be good at **Stealth**, so she picks that as her other skill at Good (+3). For her three
skills at Fair (+2), she selects **Burglary**, **Culture**, and **Athletics**. For her remaining skills at Average (+1),
she selects **Fight**, **Will**, **Notice**, and **Investigate**._

_Having decided that **Empathy** is one of Annie's best skills, Emily proposes to the GM that she change the background
stunt that lets her use **Culture** instead of **Empathy** to read a crowd to one that lets her use **Culture** instead
of **Notice** to spot hidden caches or useful junk. The GM agrees._

_Jim selects **Mechanics** at Great (+4), since that is the skill that will govern making explosives. He selects **Science**
at Good (+3), reasoning that Fizzbin should have a good understanding of chemistry, and takes **Culture** as his other skill
at Good. At Fair (+2), he selects **Investigate**, **Notice**, and **Physique** (he thinks he may need the extra physical
stress boxes). Looking at what Emily has left at Mediocre (+0) for Annie, he picks **Contacts**, **Shoot**, **Pilot**,
and **Deceive** for his four skills at Average (+1)._

# Approaches to Character Creation
The steps given so far assume a traditional approach to creating a character, in which the beginning character details
are fully developed, but it is possible to defer some character creation and develop during play.
## Session Zero
A session zero can be used to develop the characters' backgrounds and their relationships with the other characters. When
using a session zero, only the high concept and trouble are determined beforehand. Each player writes a brief description
of their first true adventure, and creates an aspect based on it. The stories are then redistributed, and the players
each give themselves a role in another character's story, whether to render assistance, or add a complication, or otherwise
affect the way the story goes. The player then creates an aspect based on that role. The last step is then
repeated (assuming at least three players), with the caveat that no character can contribute to the same story more than once.
## Deferring Details Until Session One
Another approach is to defer some or all skills, stunts, or aspects (other than the _High Concept_ and the _Trouble_) until
the first session. When a skill is first used, the player can decide where it goes on the character sheet and write it in.
If a situation suggests a stunt that seems like it fits the character, the player adds it to the sheet. Aspects likewise
may suggest themselves through interaction with the setting.
